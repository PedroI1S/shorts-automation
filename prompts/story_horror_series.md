Write ONE original English first-person horror story told across exactly 3 or 4
Shorts, as requested. Plan the complete arc and its ending before writing Part 1.
This is a continuous story, not several standalone stories with the same props.
Each part is heard once, aloud, often by someone who has not seen the others.

## The arc

Keep one protagonist, the same narrator gender and voice, the same central threat,
consistent rules, chronology and character knowledge throughout. Use at most one
other named character across the whole arc. Maintain a short continuity bible
naming the protagonist, setting, threat, the rule the threat follows, the planted
clue and the final payoff.

Give the threat one concrete rule the listener can learn (what it wants, what it
can or cannot do, what makes it come closer). Reveal the rule through events, test
it in the middle parts and use it in the finale. Never add new powers late to
manufacture a climax. A supernatural threat stays supernatural; an ordinary
villain must plausibly explain every apparently impossible event.

Every part must change the situation: a new discovery, a narrowed escape, a cost,
or a previous detail becoming more frightening. If a part could be cut without
losing anything, rewrite it. Plant the clue in Part 1 in a way that seems
harmless; the finale must show what it really meant.

## Openings

Part 1's first sentence is the most disturbing concrete thing that happens before
the story turns, stated plainly ("The baby monitor said my name in my own voice.").
Establish who is speaking, where they are and what they want through events, not
biography. Never open with "I never believed", "It was a normal night", "My name
is", "Let me tell you", "This happened", "To this day" or similar warm-up.

Parts 2 onward answer the previous cliffhanger within their first two sentences,
with at most one short line of context woven into the action, so a first-time
listener can follow. Do not spend a Short on recap.

## Cliffhangers and the finale

End EVERY nonfinal part on a specific new danger, discovery or forced decision
that the next part must answer: something the narrator sees, hears, learns or
chooses. Not a vague question, not a cut-off sentence, not withholding a fact
the narrator already knows, not an unrelated villain.

The final part resolves the central danger by using the rule and the planted
clue. Its last one or two sentences show a clear, immediate consequence or what
an earlier detail really meant. It may leave an unsettling implication, but it
must not advertise a next episode or replace the resolution with another
unanswered question.

## Voice and style

Write as someone recounting the experience aloud: short varied sentences,
concrete sensory details (sound, temperature, light, smell), natural spoken
rhythm. The protagonist acts sensibly; show why sensible actions fail instead of
making them behave stupidly to advance the plot.

Avoid: random spooky objects, sudden family secrets, "it was me all along",
dreams, "I was already dead", "everything went black", vague "something was
wrong", hands around the throat, a generic monster lunge, purple prose, gore for
shock value, stage directions, ellipses and em dashes. Avoid flat exposition such
as "I'm the night guard, and I needed to get out". Do not repeat the premise,
props, threat or ending of any recent arc supplied alongside the premise.

There is no minimum word count or duration. Never pad. Each individual script
must fit the supplied per-Short word limit. No spoken part labels, subscribe
requests or other marketing: the renderer adds part labels and a continuation
cue on screen, and the uploader supplies searchable numbered titles. Do not claim
a next part is already public. Do not present fictional events as verified real
events.

## Private check before returning

Each cliffhanger is answered in the next part's first two sentences; nobody
changes identity or gender; no character knows an unseen event; actions are
physically possible; the rule never changes; the finale pays off the planted clue
without the listener inventing missing facts.
