Write an original English first-person horror story for a spoken Short.
There is no minimum duration or word count. Let the story's quality determine
its length: never pad a complete story to reach a time target. Keep it within
the supplied maximum word count and preserve a clear setup, escalation and payoff.
The listener must understand it on the first listen and feel an immediate threat.

Open with a concrete, disturbing event in the first sentence. Establish who is
speaking, where they are and what they want without a biography or slow setup.
Use one protagonist, at most one other named character, one location and one
central threat. Every sentence must change the situation, narrow an escape or
make a previous detail more frightening. Give the protagonist a sensible action;
show why it fails instead of making them behave stupidly to advance the plot.

Plant one specific detail that matters again at the end. The final one or two
sentences must show a clear, immediate consequence or reveal what an earlier
detail really meant. A twist is optional; a frightening payoff is mandatory.
Leave the origin of the horror unexplained if needed, but never leave the basic
events or physical danger confusing. A supernatural story can stay supernatural.
An ordinary villain must plausibly account for every apparently impossible event.
Never force a mundane explanation onto supernatural evidence.
Stay with the central threat: do not add unrelated invisible forces, magic alarm
buttons or arbitrary new powers in the last lines to manufacture a climax.

Avoid random spooky objects, unrelated last-second villains, sudden family
secrets, generic "it was me all along", dreams, "I was already dead", vague
"something was wrong", and endings that only pose another question. No purple
prose, gore for shock value, stage directions, ellipses or em dashes. Write as
someone recounting an experience aloud, with short varied sentences and concrete
sensory details. Do not claim the fiction is a verified real event.
Do not end with fingers/hands around the narrator's throat or a generic monster
lunge. The payoff should be specific to this story's established threat. Avoid
flat exposition such as "I'm the night guard, and I needed to get out"; make the
role and stakes clear through events. Do not repeat the props or ending of a
recent story supplied alongside the premise.

Privately check the timeline, character knowledge and physical actions before
returning the story. If the payoff requires the listener to invent missing facts,
rewrite it. Mark protagonist/narrator gender accurately for voice selection.
